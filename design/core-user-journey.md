# Core User Journey

Starting with an empty database, Wyatt registers a FriendlyFive account and proposes a bet to Alex's email, naming their friend Morgan by email as the third-party resolver. Neither Alex nor Morgan needs an account yet. The claim says that MIT football will win its game today by more than seven points, including overtime, with the loser owing the winner lunch up to $15. Wyatt specifies that a canceled game creates no obligation, sets a reporting cutoff after the game, and chooses the ESPN box score as the resolution rule. FriendlyFive gives Wyatt separate invitation links to share privately with Alex and Morgan. Each opens their own link and chooses a username and password. Morgan can register first but waits to see the agreement until the participants accept. Alex reviews the initial version, including the margin, stakes, cutoff, and Morgan as resolver, then accepts before the cutoff. No extra revision is needed to add Morgan. MIT wins by exactly seven points. After the cutoff, Wyatt mistakenly reports that the claim is true and attaches the ESPN score as optional evidence. Alex disputes the report with the required explanation that seven points does not satisfy “more than seven.” Morgan signs in and reviews the accepted terms, report, and dispute, then records that the claim is false with an explanation of the seven-point margin. FriendlyFive finalizes the outcome and records that Wyatt owes Alex lunch. Wyatt buys Alex Chipotle and reports completion with an optional explanation and receipt photo. Alex reviews the completion report and confirms receipt. Both participants see the agreement as complete. Morgan can review the final outcome but cannot access their private fulfillment details. The friends finish lunch with a shared understanding of the terms and result, showing how an agreed resolver can help settle a disagreement without processing the stake through the app.
