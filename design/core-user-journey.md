# Core User Journey

Starting with an empty database, Wyatt registers a FriendlyFive account with a unique email and username, then proposes a bet to Alex's email even though Alex does not yet have an account. The bet says that MIT football will win its game today, with the loser owing the winner lunch. Wyatt specifies that a canceled game results in a push, sets a reporting cutoff, chooses the ESPN box score as the resolution rule, and leaves the optional resolver blank. FriendlyFive reserves Alex's email and gives Wyatt an invitation link to share. Alex opens the link, chooses his own username and password for the invited email, and is taken directly to the private proposal. He reviews the terms and accepts the agreement. After the reporting cutoff passes, Wyatt reports that MIT won the game, explains the result, and attaches a screenshot of the ESPN result as optional evidence. Alex signs back in, reviews the report and photo, and confirms that the claim is true. FriendlyFive finalizes the outcome and records that Alex owes Wyatt lunch. Alex then buys Wyatt Chipotle and reports the obligation complete with an explanation and optional receipt photo. Wyatt signs in again, reviews the completion report and optional receipt photo, and confirms receipt. The dashboard shows the agreement as complete, demonstrating how FriendlyFive helps friends establish accounts from an invitation, agree on clear terms, determine an outcome, and confirm fulfillment without confusion or disagreement.
